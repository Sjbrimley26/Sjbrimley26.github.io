Hi, I'm Spencer Brimley. For the last four years I ran an 11-person door and trim distributor — sales, purchasing, bookkeeping, scheduling — and on the side I built the software the company ran on. The business worked on paper and QuickBooks; nothing tracked an order between the estimate and the delivery, so in 2022 I started building one: first a task board, then a full ERP designed around how each person worked. It ended up as 31 tables, every catalog item mapped to vendors' part numbers, 29 automations keeping QuickBooks, the shop floor and the customers in sync — about 1,100 automation runs a day by the end. The case study tells the whole story, including what I'd do differently.

Everything I've built, I built while running a business, not as a job title. Learning n8n, Baserow and Python happened because backorders weren't being passed on and bills were slipping through, not from a course. The same is true of the homelab behind this site: about twenty services on Linux, a PostgreSQL database, backups, VPNs, reverse proxies — and a local LLM bench on a 4 GB GPU (small-gpu-bench).

I'm more of a backend person than a front-end one — the honest proof is that this site is hand-written HTML and CSS, on purpose, hosting the real work instead of hiding it behind a template.

Before the distributor I processed loans, ran a fraud-and-eligibility QC team for an emergency-rental-assistance program, and sold doors — so explaining technical tools to people who don't want to learn them is, historically, the job.

Outside work: carpentry (Spencer B Carpentry LLC), oyster and lion's mane mushrooms, Mandarin Chinese — upper-intermediate, prepping for HSK 5.
